B · Compact hero

The news carousel is a strip, not a stage: 176 px of 656 px, about a quarter of the main area. Everything else belongs to the dashboard — roughly nine grid rows without scrolling, so two rows of tiles fit and the grid reads as a canvas the user is meant to fill. The same two v1 modules are placed here as in A and C, deliberately unchanged, which is why the free space below them is visible: that is what v1 actually looks like.

What it gives

  • The dashboard is a real canvas: two rows of tiles fit, so the freedom the free grid promises is actually usable on a 1280×820 window.
  • The user's own layout dominates their home screen, which is what the concept's vision says the lower half is for.
  • News stay present and rotating without owning the screen.

What it costs

  • 176 px squeezes the split slide: the image is a thin band and the body text has to be cut to two lines. banner is nearly unusable at this height.
  • The launcher looks less curated — closer to a tool than to a front page.
  • With only two v1 modules the free space reads as unfinished rather than as an invitation.
